Most chimney advice is about how to repair chimneys. Sometimes the right answer is the opposite. Here's how to know when.

## When taking a chimney down makes sense

The case for chimney removal is strong when:

- **The chimney is no longer in use.** No working fireplace, no boiler flue, nothing going up it.
- **The chimney is failing badly.** Multiple spalled bricks, flaunching gone, pots cracked. Repair cost would be substantial.
- **The chimney is causing leaks.** Failed flashing at the chimney-to-roof junction is one of the most common sources of roof leaks.
- **The property is not listed and the chimney doesn't add value.** Most buyers don't value a redundant chimney. Some specifically don't want one (cost of ongoing maintenance).

## When keeping the chimney makes sense

- **The chimney is in use.** Working fireplace, wood burner, or boiler flue. Don't take down a working chimney.
- **The property is listed or in a conservation area.** Listed building consent is usually required to take down a chimney, and Bath being Bath, most of central Bath is in a conservation area. We always check before recommending removal.
- **The chimney has architectural value.** A prominent stack that gives the property its character. Often true on Victorian terraces.
- **The chimney is in good condition.** If pointing and flaunching are fine, there's no urgent reason to remove.

## What "taking it down" actually means

Taking a chimney down to roof level means:

1. **Strip the chimney pots** — usually these can be reused if in good condition
2. **Remove the upper courses of brickwork** — down to just below the roof line, often to where the stack enters the roof
3. **Cap the flues** — at the new (lower) top of the stack, with a concrete cap or similar
4. **Renew the roof covering where the stack was** — the roof timbers around the chimney usually need new flashing and often new tiles or slates
5. **Remove the lower part of the chimney inside the roof** — usually down to the ceiling below
6. **Vent the remaining flue** — so any residual moisture can escape

What you're left with is a small chimney pot or capped stack at roof level — visible from the upstairs windows, not visible from the street. The chimney breast inside the house can usually be left in place, just with no flue above it.

## What it costs

Removing a chimney to roof level is a medium-sized job — usually 2-3 days for the strip and roof work, plus internal making-good. The cost is mostly access (scaffolding) and disposal (skip).

We're not publishing a number because every chimney is different. But as a rule of thumb, if a full chimney repair (rebuild + repoint + re-flaunch + new flashing) would cost the same or more than a removal, the removal starts to make sense.

## What about the inside of the chimney?

Below the roof, the chimney usually continues down through the house to the ground floor (or wherever the original fireplace was). After removing the part above the roof:

- The chimney breast on the upper floor is still there, with the flue now capped
- The original fireplace recess can be left as-is (a decorative feature), filled in, or reopened if you want a working fireplace again
- The lower flue (where the original fireplace was) usually needs to be vented at the bottom if it's still open

For a typical Bath Victorian terrace, this internal work is straightforward and the builders can usually do it after we've done the roof part.

## Common concerns

**"Will it affect my house insurance?"**

Usually no, but you should tell your insurer. The structure of the house isn't affected by removing an unused chimney. Your buildings insurance premium is unlikely to change.

**"Will it make my house harder to sell?"**

For a Bath property, usually no. Most buyers don't value a redundant chimney that needs ongoing maintenance. A few buyers specifically want working fireplaces — if your property has good period detail elsewhere, this rarely matters.

**"Will it affect the value of my house?"**

Generally neutral to slightly positive. Removing the maintenance burden of a chimney usually helps value. The aesthetic change (smaller stack at roof level) is rarely visible from the street.

## What we usually recommend

For a Bath property with a chimney that's no longer in use and needs significant repair, we usually recommend getting a quote for both:

1. **Repair** — repoint, re-flaunch, replace pots, renew flashing. Gets you another 20-30 years.
2. **Removal** — take it down to roof level. No more chimney maintenance forever.

Then weigh the costs and pick the one that suits. There's no wrong answer.
