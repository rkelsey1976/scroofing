A roof rarely goes from "fine" to "leaking badly" in one step. There are almost always signs first — small, easy to ignore, easy to put off. The trick is knowing which signs matter.

## Inside the house

**Ceiling stains that appear after rain and disappear afterwards** are the classic early signal. The water is finding its way in during rain and drying out between showers. By the time a stain is permanently dark, the leak has been running for a while.

**Paint bubbling or peeling on upstairs walls** — particularly on external walls, particularly near the ceiling line. Often mistaken for condensation. Usually isn't.

**A musty smell in the upstairs rooms or loft** that you can't pin on anything specific. Mould needs moisture. If you've got mould and you can't see where the moisture is coming from, look up.

**Light visible in the loft from outside** — go up on a sunny day, close the loft hatch, and see if any daylight is getting through the roof. If it is, water can get in too.

## Outside the house

You don't need to get on the roof to see most of these. Binoculars or the zoom on your phone camera are enough.

**Tiles or slates that have slipped out of line** — the roof line should be straight and uniform. If you can see one tile that's lower, higher, or at a different angle, something's moved.

**Cracked or missing mortar on the ridge** — the ridge is the top course of tiles or the capping stones along the apex. Mortar there should be intact. If you can see gaps, that's where water's getting in.

**Sagging in the roofline** — a small dip or bow in what should be a straight line. This is more serious and means structural movement, not just a slipped tile. Worth a call sooner rather than later.

**Green staining running down the wall below the gutter** — usually means the gutter's overflowing or the fascia's failing. Not a roof problem yet, but if left it becomes one.

## In the loft

If you can get into the loft safely, go up there after a rainy day and have a look with a torch.

**Wet timber** — rafters, purlins, or the back of the roof decking should be dry. Damp wood feels cold and often looks darker. Dry wood feels warmer and lighter.

**Drips or puddles on the loft floor insulation** — the insulation will be wet in a clearly localised spot, not generally damp throughout.

**Compressed or matted insulation** — sometimes a sign that someone's been walking in the loft and has packed the insulation down. Compressed insulation doesn't perform. Worth knowing if you don't want to replace it.

## The ones that don't matter (yet)

A few things look alarming but are usually benign:

- **Moss on the tiles** — usually cosmetic on a pitched roof. Worth keeping an eye on if it's very heavy, but not urgent.
- **A single missing tile on a calm day** — annoying, not catastrophic. The felt underneath should keep things dry for a while.
- **Discolouration on chimney pots** — they fade with age.

## What to do when you've spotted something

Take a photo and note roughly where it is on the house ("front bedroom ceiling, north-east corner", say). If it's an internal sign, mention when it appeared and what the weather was doing at the time.
